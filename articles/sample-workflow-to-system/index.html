Sample article — How a small workflow becomes a useful system

A publishing workflow sample that follows an illustrative handoff from exploring options to choosing a scope and testing a useful system.

By Sample author · Published October 11, 2026

  • Publishing sample
  • Workflows

This is sample content for reviewing the publishing workflow. The scenario is illustrative; it is not a client story, a record of delivered results, or a claim about a named author's experience.

A team copies information from one tool to another. Before choosing software, it helps to understand what moves, who checks it, and what happens when a step fails.

Explore the work

Start with one real handoff. Ask the people doing it what takes time and where information gets lost. Consider several options:

  • Remove an unnecessary step.
  • Connect existing tools through an integration.
  • Build a small tool for a gap that the existing systems cannot cover.

Kheya's services describe different ways to approach these needs. Technology is useful when it fits the work and its constraints.

Choose a testable scope

Pick a small enough step to examine properly. Agree the inputs, the expected output and who can approve the action. A simple acceptance note might say:

Given an approved input, the workflow creates one correct record and shows the person responsible whether it succeeded.

Record what should happen if the destination is unavailable. A retry must not create another copy of the same record.

Approved input → checked transformation → destination → visible result

Make it useful

Try representative examples, including missing fields and failed connections. Keep the people using the workflow involved. Document who owns access, how to correct an error, and how to stop the automation.

The result can then be judged against the criteria agreed at the start. It may also reveal a better next question. The Kheya approach explains the sequence of exploring, choosing and making the pieces work together.

Find a starting point

If you have a workflow in mind, use the project diagnostic or start a conversation. Bring an example of the current steps and the outcome you need.
